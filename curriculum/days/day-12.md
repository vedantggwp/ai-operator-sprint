## Lesson


Admin work is mostly transformation: raw material in, shaped output out. Meeting notes become action items. A rough ask becomes a formal request. A messy inbox becomes a prioritised list. The inputs arrive in different forms every time; the outputs need to be in consistent shapes.


That's exactly where Claude is useful, and exactly where most people stop short. They use it for the occasional email and then go back to doing everything else manually. An Admin OS is the difference. It's a set of named, tested workflows for your regular administrative tasks, run from Sprint HQ with a brief that takes 30 seconds to fill.


The 3 most common admin transformations in knowledge work are: notes to actions (meetings and calls produce obligations that get lost), inbox to priorities (a full email thread needs a one-paragraph decision summary), and rough to polished (a voice note or bullet list needs to become a professional message). Each of these has a standard shape. Today you build the template for each shape.


The goal is not automating admin. It's removing the friction from admin you'll keep doing. Thirty seconds to fill a brief, two minutes to review the output, done. That's admin that takes five minutes instead of twenty-five.


## Build


1. **Identify your 3 biggest admin repetitions (1 min).** Think about last week. What admin work appeared more than once? The Monday morning emails? The weekly status update? Post-meeting task extraction? Pick the top 3. (Don't have 3? Use the 3 from the lesson above as your starting point.)


2. **Run the Notes-to-Actions template (3 min).** In Sprint HQ, open a new chat and paste some real (or dummy) meeting notes:


   ```
   Admin OS: Notes to actions


   Context: [1 sentence about the meeting: who attended, what it was about]
   Notes: [paste your notes, however rough]
   My role in this meeting: [attendee / facilitator / note-taker]


   Extract:
   1. Every action item, with the name of the person responsible if mentioned
   2. Every decision made (not discussed, actually decided)
   3. Any open question that needs a follow-up before the next meeting


   Format: 3 labelled sections, bullet list in each, under 150 words total. Skip anything uncertain; flag it as "needs clarification".
   ```


   Run this on notes from a recent meeting. Compare to what you'd have written in 10 minutes.


3. **Build the Inbox-to-Priority template (3 min).** For a thread or chain of emails you're dealing with:


   ```
   Admin OS: Inbox to priority


   Thread context: [paste the email chain, or a summary of it]
   What decision is pending: [the thing that needs to happen]
   Who needs to decide: [you / your manager / the team]


   Give me:
   1. One-paragraph summary of the thread for someone who hasn't read it
   2. The single most important thing that needs to happen next, in one sentence
   3. Any information missing before a decision can be made
   ```


4. **Build the Rough-to-Polished template (2 min).** In Sprint HQ:


   ```
   Admin OS: Rough to polished


   Content: [paste your rough notes, bullet points, or voice-note transcript]
   Audience: [who receives this]
   Format needed: [email / Slack message / formal memo / brief report]
   Tone: [the tone appropriate for this audience]
   Length: [target word count]


   Constraints: match my WRITING-OS style anchor. No corporate filler. No "I hope this finds you well".
   ```


5. **Add all 3 to WRITING-OS (1 min).** Paste them as a new section titled "Admin OS" and save. You now have 6 templates in WRITING-OS: 3 writing modes, 3 file-task modes, and now 3 admin modes.

## If it goes wrong


- **The meeting notes are too sparse for Claude to extract from.** Good to know now. The template reveals the quality of your notes as much as it processes them. Going forward, add one line at the top of your notes: "Decisions:" and "Actions:". Even rough markers help enormously.
- **The inbox summary misses what I think is most important.** You probably know something the email thread doesn't say. Add a line to the template: "Background I know that isn't in the thread: [1 sentence]." Claude can't read your context; you have to supply it.
- **The polished version sounds too formal (or too casual).** That's the tone line doing its job, or not doing it. Change "professional" to "direct and conversational", or "formal" to "the register of a board memo, not an academic paper". Adjectives guide; specific examples train.
- **I already have systems for these.** Good. The Admin OS is not a replacement for your calendar app or your task manager. It's the transformation layer: raw input to shaped output. If you already do that well manually, skip the template and note why in WRITING-OS. Your system is the point; the template is just the form it takes.
