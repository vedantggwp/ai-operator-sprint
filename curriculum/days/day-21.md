## Lesson


This is ship day 3, and it's different from the previous two. Days 7 and 14 shipped documents: a prompt library, a system map. Today you ship a running automation. Something that processes input and produces output, repeatable, with a time-saved number you calculated yourself.


The honest version of Week 3: you built Automation 1 on Day 16 (a template that processes one of your delegation tasks), practiced free building on Day 19, and spent Days 17-18 setting up infrastructure. Some of that will have worked well; some of it will have been rougher than expected. Ship day doesn't pretend otherwise.


What you're shipping today is the best running automation you have from this week, with a real measurement of what it saves. "It saves me time" is not a measurement. "It converts my Monday meeting notes to a formatted status update in about 90 seconds instead of 15 minutes" is a measurement. The number matters because it turns a tool into a business case. When you demo on Day 30, the time-saved number is what makes the person watching actually believe it.


Pick your strongest automation from the week. Run it one more time on real inputs. Time yourself. Write the number down. That number goes on the ship record.


## Build


1. **Pick your best automation (1 min).** Look at what you built across Days 16 and 19. Which one: runs cleanly on real inputs, produces output you'd actually use, and could run every week without needing you to rebuild it? That's the one to ship.


2. **Time a full run (3 min).** Take your chosen automation and run it end-to-end on real inputs. Time from when you open Claude Code to when you have a usable output. Write that number down. Then estimate how long the same task took you manually before the sprint. The difference is your time-saved number.


3. **Write the ship record (3 min).** In Sprint HQ:


   ```
   Help me write a ship record for Automation 2.


   Automation: [name]
   What it does: [one sentence]
   Input: [what you put in]
   Output: [what comes out]
   Time to run: [your measurement]
   Time saved vs manual: [your estimate]
   Reliability: [has it worked consistently, or does it need babysitting?]
   Known limitations: [what it can't do or gets wrong]


   Write this as a 150-word record that I could hand to someone else and they'd know what to do with it. Include a "how to run it" section.
   ```


4. **Update the SPRINT-TRACKER (1 min).** Open your `SPRINT-TRACKER` and add the row for Day 21. Mark the weekly time-saved total from all automations (Day 16's, Day 19's, and today's if they're different). The number, even if it's 20 minutes, is the record.


5. **Add it to the AI-OS Map (1 min).** Open your `AI-OS-MAP` and add one line to the tools section: "Automation: [name], saves [X] minutes/week." That line is the proof that the map connects to something real.

## If it goes wrong


- **None of my automations ran cleanly this week.** Ship the one that ran least badly. The ship record has a "known limitations" field for a reason. A shipped imperfect tool beats an unshipped aspirational one. Write what went wrong in the limitations section; that's where it belongs.
- **I can't calculate the time saved because I haven't been tracking.** Estimate. Think about the last time you did the task manually and how long it took. You don't need a precise number; you need an order of magnitude. "About 15 minutes instead of 2 hours" is enough for a ship record.
- **The automation needs me to babysit it every run.** That's reliability 3/5, not 0/5. Write it honestly: "Runs in 90 seconds but needs review before the output is sent." That's a working tool with a known gap; it's shippable. The gap is what Week 4 may address.
- **Day 19's build didn't work and Day 16's template is the only thing I have.** Then Day 16 is what you ship. One working automation, shipped with a real number, is a good Week 3. The programme doesn't require a portfolio; it requires something real.
