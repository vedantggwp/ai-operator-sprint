## Lesson


You've built a working thing. Now you make it legible to people who weren't there.


A proof page is not a portfolio. A portfolio is a collection. A proof page is a single argument: here is what I built, here is that it works, here is what it means about what I'm capable of. It takes 10 minutes to make and it travels: you can link to it in an email, share it in a message, or hand it to someone after the demo.


The simplest proof page is a public document, a Notion page, a GitHub README, or a single web page. The format doesn't matter. What matters is that someone can land on it, read it in under two minutes, and come away knowing: what you made, that it's real, and why it's useful. Three things. Not ten.


You already have everything you need for this. The Demo Spec names the thing and why it matters. The README explains how it works. Yesterday's notes (if you took the optional step) have a 30-second video of it running. Today you assemble these into one linkable place.


The proof page is also preparation for the Day 28 story. Writing down "what I made and why" in public forces you to find words for the experience. Those words become the case study. The act of making it legible makes it more valuable.


## Build


1. **Choose your format (1 min).** You need somewhere that produces a link:
   - A Notion page (if you use Notion)
   - A GitHub Gist or repository README (public: anyone with the link can see it)
   - A Google Doc set to "Anyone with the link can view"
   - A simple page on a platform you already use (LinkedIn article, Medium draft)


   Pick whichever takes the least setup. The format is not the point.


2. **Draft the proof page (5 min).** In Sprint HQ:


   ```
   Help me write my proof page for [demo name]. Use the DEMO-SPEC in project knowledge.


   The proof page needs exactly these 5 elements:
   1. A title: "[Demo name]: what I built in [programme name]"
   2. The thing in one paragraph: what it does, who it's for, and the problem it solves
   3. Proof it works: 3 bullet points from your actual experience using it (be specific: "it converts 300-word meeting notes to a 5-point action list in under 2 minutes")
   4. What I used to build it: tools, days in the programme, approximate total time
   5. What's next: one sentence on where this goes from here


   Plain English, under 250 words total. Write it for someone who doesn't know what the AI Operator Sprint is.
   ```


3. **Edit for honesty (2 min).** Read it once. Check the "proof it works" section: is each bullet point something you actually verified, or something that sounds plausible? Replace any bullet that's hypothetical ("this could save...") with one that's observed ("in my test it saved..."). The proof needs to be proof.


4. **Publish it (1 min).** Paste it into your chosen platform. Get a link. Paste that link into your `SPRINT-TRACKER` next to Day 27.


5. **Add it to the Demo Spec (1 min).** Open `DEMO-SPEC` and add one line at the bottom: "Proof page: [link]." Now when your Day 30 person asks "is there somewhere I can share this?", you have an answer.

## If it goes wrong


- **I don't have proof it works beyond my own use.** Your own use is enough. "I ran this on my Monday meeting notes every week for the last 2 weeks and it saved me about 15 minutes each time" is specific, honest, and more convincing than a testimonial. First-person evidence counts.
- **The platform feels too public.** Use a password-protected link or set the doc to "anyone with the link". You don't have to post it anywhere. The proof page is ready when someone asks, not necessarily broadcasting to the world.
- **The draft sounds like marketing.** Check the "proof it works" section. If the bullets say things like "dramatically reduces" or "effortlessly handles", rewrite them in measured language: "reduces from about 20 minutes to about 3", "handles single-topic notes well; multi-topic notes sometimes need a second pass". Specific is more credible than superlative.
- **I don't know what "what's next" should say.** Write the honest version: "I'm going to run this for one more month to see if the time savings hold, and then decide whether to expand it." An honest next step is better than an aspirational one.


## Going deeper (optional)


Add a screenshot or output sample to the proof page: the actual output from one real run, with any sensitive data removed. Visual proof is more convincing than verbal proof. A before-and-after (raw notes vs formatted output) is even better. Skippable. The 5-element page is the artefact.
