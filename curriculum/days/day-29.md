## Lesson


A demo is a performance, and performances get better with rehearsal. Not polished and scripted: practiced enough that you know what to say, in what order, without having to look at notes.


The structure of a good 2-minute demo is very simple. What the problem was, in one sentence. What you built to solve it, in one sentence. Here is it working (the live demonstration). What this means about what's possible, in one sentence. That's it. Four sentences and a live run. The live run is the centre of it; the sentences are the frame.


Recording a 2-minute version serves two purposes. You get the rehearsal repetitions (recording forces full runs; talking to yourself stops short). And you produce an asset: a screen recording you can send to anyone who couldn't be there, or who wants to show someone else, or who you meet in 6 months and want to remind of what you built.


You don't need professional equipment. The screen recording tool that came with your computer is enough. The mic on your laptop or earphones is enough. The goal is a recording someone could watch without squinting, not a production.


The most common mistake in demo recordings is spending the first 30 seconds explaining rather than showing. Start by showing the input going in and the output coming out. Explain what they're watching as it runs. That sequence is both more interesting and more honest: the demo earns its description, rather than promising a description it then has to justify.


## Build


1. **Write the 4-sentence script (2 min).** In Sprint HQ or a note:


   ```
   Help me write a 4-sentence demo script for [demo name].


   Sentence 1: The problem, in plain English (what you were doing before, and why it was slow or painful)
   Sentence 2: What you built (what it is, one sentence)
   Sentence 3: [This is the live demo moment. Don't script it; just note: "run the demo here"]
   Sentence 4: What this means (what's now possible that wasn't before, in one sentence)


   Write it in first person, at the pace of normal speech. Read it aloud and cut any word that slows you down. Under 60 words total (excluding the demo moment).
   ```


2. **Do one practice run, no recording (2 min).** Open your `demo-build` folder and your script. Talk through the demo once, out loud, following the 4 sentences. Don't start over if you stumble. Note what you stumbled on: that's what needs one more clarification.


3. **Record the 2-minute version (4 min).** Open your screen recording tool:
   - Mac: Shift+Cmd+5, then "Record Selected Portion"
   - Windows: Win+G opens the Game Bar, which has a screen recorder
   - Or: Zoom or any video call tool, share your screen, record to local computer


   Hit record. Say sentence 1. Run the demo while narrating. Say sentence 4. Stop recording. Don't worry about perfection: a slightly imperfect recording that exists is the goal. If you want a second take, take one, but 2 takes maximum.


   <!-- FACTCHECK: Verify current screen recording shortcuts for Mac and Windows. Mac shortcut (Shift+Cmd+5) is longstanding but check. Windows Game Bar (Win+G) verify current availability -->


4. **Watch it back (1 min).** Watch the recording once. Note one thing you'd change. Don't re-record: change the note. The recording exists. That's the artifact.


5. **Send it to one person (1 min).** Not your Day 30 person: someone whose opinion you trust. Send the recording and say: "2-minute demo. What's the one thing that would make it more convincing?" You want the answer before tomorrow.

## If it goes wrong


- **The recording is longer than 2 minutes.** Cut the intro. Most demos open with 30 seconds of context the viewer doesn't need yet. Start with the input going in. That cut alone takes most demos under 2 minutes.
- **I stumbled on the same sentence twice.** That sentence has a problem. Rewrite it shorter. If you can't say it fluently in practice, you won't say it fluently in front of your Day 30 person. One short clear sentence is better than one long correct one.
- **I hate watching myself on video.** Watch it on mute first, just to see what the screen looks like. Then listen without watching. Then watch and listen together. Three passes at lower stakes than one full watch. After the second pass the self-consciousness usually fades.
- **I can't get the screen recording to work.** Use a phone to record the screen instead. It's less clean but it's a recording, and a recording that exists beats a plan to record. Or do a live demo tomorrow without the pre-recording: a live demo is often more compelling anyway.
