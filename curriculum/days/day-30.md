## Lesson


Thirty days. Thirty small builds. One person, one demo, one conversation.


The aim of the whole sprint was never to become an expert. It was to get to Day 30 with something real and show it to someone whose opinion you care about. You did that. Everything else: the prompt library, the OS map, the automations, the case study, the proof page, the 2-minute video, these are the evidence of what 10 minutes a day builds.


Today is not a test. It's a conversation. The person you're showing this to isn't grading you. They're watching someone they know show them something they made. That's a different situation from a presentation: it's closer to showing a friend something you're working on. The best demos feel like that.


One thing you may notice: the conversation may go off-script. They may ask a question you didn't prepare for. They may focus on something you thought was peripheral. They may miss the thing you thought was obvious. All of this is fine. The demo isn't a defence of the product. It's the start of a conversation about what's possible when you build carefully, one day at a time.


After the demo, the sprint isn't over. It's the opposite. The tools are set up, the habits are running, the system is real. Today is where the habit becomes permanent, or doesn't. The difference is one decision you'll make this evening.


## Build


1. **Set up for the demo (2 min).** Open everything you need before the conversation starts: `demo-build` open, your proof page link ready to share, your case study open, the recording ready if they ask for it. You shouldn't be navigating folders while talking.


2. **Give the demo (the 10 minutes).** Follow the 4-sentence script from Day 29. Start by showing the input going in. Walk through what happens. Show the output. Then say: "This took about 30 days of 10 minutes each to get to this point. Here's what I built along the way." Share the proof page link.


   Then stop talking. Let them respond. What they say next is the most interesting part of the demo.


3. **After the demo (2 min).** Write down:
   - What they said when you stopped talking
   - One thing that surprised you in the conversation
   - One thing you'd do differently in a future demo


   Don't lose this. It's the input for the next thing you build.


4. **Update the SPRINT-TRACKER (2 min).** Add the final entry: "Day 30: demo given. Person: [name]. Their response: [one sentence]. What I'd do differently: [one sentence]." The tracker is now complete.


5. **Make the decision (1 min).** There's one decision to make this evening. Not whether to keep using AI (you're already using it). Whether to keep the sprint going. Thirty-one days from now, will you have something else to demo, to the same person or a different one? A yes is an answer. A not-yet is also an answer. Write it in your `COMMITMENT` file. The loop closes when you write it down.

## If it goes wrong


- **The person cancelled.** Show it to a different person today. Anyone works: a family member, a colleague, a friend. The constraint is showing it to someone today, on Day 30. The named person can see it on another day; the date is what matters.
- **The demo crashed during the session.** Tell the truth: "It broke, which is normal for a first version." Show it in a different way: walk through the steps in plain English. Show the output from yesterday's test. The competence on display is that you built it and you can talk about it clearly, not that it never fails.
- **They didn't seem impressed.** That's not failure. Impress is a high bar and hard to read. What matters: did they understand what you built? Did they ask a question that suggests they were thinking about how to use something like this? Either of those is a win. Note what they said verbatim, not your interpretation of their reaction.
- **I didn't finish everything in the spec.** Demo what's done. Every demo in the history of technology has been of something unfinished. What you demoed is real. What's not finished is the roadmap.


## Going deeper (optional)

Not a listicle: a letter. Seal it in your `AI-OS` folder and open it in 6 months. It's the best record you'll have of what this month actually was. Skippable. The demo is the artefact.
